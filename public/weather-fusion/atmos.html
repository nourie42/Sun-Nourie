      How it really feels

      Sun, wind and moisture

      The thermometer is only part of the story. Sunshine, wind and moisture change how warm or cold it feels when you are out in it.

      The science of feels-like

      Feels-like is the site’s custom outdoor estimate. It starts with UTCI, which combines air temperature, moisture, wind and an estimated sun load, then gradually lifts colder results toward the NWS wind-chill equation when that result is warmer. The standard NWS index is intended for 50°F or below with wind above 3 mph; the site’s fade through 60°F and 2–4 mph is a custom smoothing rule. “In the shade” takes the direct sun estimate out.

      Dew point is the temperature at which the air would be saturated. It measures the actual moisture in the air, which is why it tracks how sticky it feels better than relative humidity does. Wet bulb uses Stull’s approximation.

      Risk labels apply UTCI-derived heat-stress thresholds of 78.8°, 89.6° and 100.4°F to that custom estimate. They are estimates, not official heat alerts.
