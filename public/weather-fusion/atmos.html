      How it really feels

      The thermometer is only part of the story. Sunshine, wind and moisture change how warm or cold it feels when you are out in it.

      The science of feels-like

      Feels-like is the site’s outdoor estimate of the Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI). It combines air temperature, moisture, wind and an estimated sun load, with the NWS wind chill as a floor in cold wind. “In the shade” takes the sun out.

      Dew point is the temperature at which the air would be saturated. It measures the actual moisture in the air, which is why it tracks how sticky it feels better than relative humidity does. Wet bulb uses Stull’s approximation.

      The heat categories (warm, hot, very hot) follow the UTCI heat-stress limits of 78.8°, 89.6° and 100.4°F. They are estimates, not official heat alerts.
