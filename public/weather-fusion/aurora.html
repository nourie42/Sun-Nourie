How this forecast is built — the short version

Forecasters don’t use one weather model — they use several, and the models often disagree. This page starts with the National Weather Service forecast, then blends in guidance from computer models with names like HRRR, ECMWF, NBM and GEFS, using fixed weights that favor the models that tend to do best at each time range.

“Chance of rain” means the chance that measurable rain falls at your exact spot. A 40% chance does not mean rain covers 40% of the area — it means that in 10 past days with a setup like this one, it rained on about 4 of them.

Confidence here is not a probability and not a grade of the forecaster. It is a plain measure of agreement: when independent models land on nearly the same temperature and rainfall, confidence is high. When they spread out — or some data is missing — confidence drops, and we say so.

Aurora is an experimental view of the same Weather Nourie forecast — same science, friendlier words. Data: National Weather Service and model guidance, blended on weather-nourie. Temperatures in Fahrenheit.
