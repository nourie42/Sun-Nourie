About the tile graphs
Sidewalk heat and UV index

Surface temperatures are estimates for dry concrete and dark asphalt exposed to the sky. A two-layer heat-balance model integrates up to 48 hours of recent model weather, absorbed sunlight, thermal radiation from clouds and the sky, wind cooling and heat stored below the surface. Current station air temperature and available current-hour companion readings finish the calculation. Cloud-adjusted solar radiation is used directly; solar position and cloud fraction provide a fallback when radiation is missing. Clouds also slow cooling at night.

The estimated ranges vary material reflectivity, conductivity, heat capacity, wind exposure, sunlight and initial stored heat. They are engineering estimates, not measured temperatures or a validated confidence interval. Opaque cloud amount is approximated with total cloud cover. Trees, buildings, wet pavement, snow and ice can make the actual surface different. Recent weather comes from archived model forecasts, not a sidewalk sensor. Missing history is shown as unavailable.

This is surface heat, not a prediction of a dog's body temperature or a guarantee of paw safety. NWS pet guidance recommends checking the actual pavement with the back of your hand and avoiding hot asphalt and cement.

The red paw-burn banner appears when either estimated surface reaches 135°F. The amber warning appears earlier when either upper estimated range reaches 135°F. AAHA describes paw burns within a minute on 135°F asphalt; this is a danger reference, not a universal safe/unsafe cutoff. Exposure time, material and individual paws matter. The uncertainty warning is precautionary, not a prediction of a burn. No banner does not mean safe: check the actual surface and choose shade or grass when it is hot.

Peak UV is the highest forecast index for that location's entire local calendar day, including hours already passed. It stays the day's maximum on Tonight. It comes from Open-Meteo, separately from the temperature blend. The hourly strip uses that specific hour's cloud-adjusted UV forecast, not the daily maximum. Missing hourly values display a dash, while a real zero is preserved. Values are rounded to whole numbers; 0–2 Low, 3–5 Moderate, 6–7 High, 8–10 Very high, and 11+ Extreme. WHO recommends sun protection at UV 3 and above.

Methods: METRo surface energy balance · EnergyPlus sky radiation · FHWA pavement properties. The simplified model here has not been locally calibrated.

How the feels-like estimates work

Waiting for the latest weather.

Human risk banners use the site's estimated UTCI, not NWS Heat Index or an official warning. Strong heat stress begins at 89.6°F UTCI (32°C), very strong at 100.4°F (38°C), and extreme at 114.8°F (46°C). Strong cold stress begins at 8.6°F UTCI (−13°C), very strong at −16.6°F (−27°C), and extreme at −40°F (−40°C). We include the boundary conservatively and classify before display rounding. ECMWF UTCI stress categories. Shade displays its modeled feels-like temperature and has no risk banner. Sun uses its matching feels-like estimate. Risk banners appear only above the outdoor-weather and For Pets graphics. These broad estimates are not a diagnosis or a guarantee of safety; activity, exposure duration and individual vulnerability matter. Missing readings receive no invented category. Pet pavement banners use the separate surface-heat method below.

Warmest feels like today compares the current estimate with the remaining hourly forecasts before local midnight. The time is the highest remaining forecast hour; when the current estimate is warmer, both Now and the later forecast peak time are shown. This is not a history of earlier station readings. Missing future hours are disclosed; the card does not substitute tomorrow morning's low after 6 PM.

The number under the current temperature is an equivalent outdoor apparent temperature, not a claim that a person’s skin is physically at that temperature. Actual skin temperature depends on physiology, clothing, activity, acclimatization and exposure time.

One all-season fallback

Weather Nourie follows the attached Real-Feel Skin Temperature research Tier 3 approach: UTCI is the primary all-season fallback, using air temperature, moisture, wind and an estimated mean-radiant environment instead of switching between Heat Index and Wind Chill.

Dew point & wet bulb

Dew point is preferred for water-vapor pressure, so lower dew points naturally make the same air temperature feel cooler. Wet bulb is calculated separately with the Stull approximation to show evaporative-cooling potential; it is not added again because that would count moisture twice.

Sun, clouds and rain

Solar elevation and the forecast sky state are used to estimate a broad radiant environment. Clear, partly cloudy, overcast, rainy, stormy, snowy, foggy and nighttime hours therefore use different radiant assumptions. This is a Tier 3 modeled equivalent temperature, not measured sunlight, measured skin temperature, or validated EPST.

Urban streets

Pavement, building shade and street-canyon winds can change what one block feels like. The nearby observed temperature already captures some broad local influence, but Weather Nourie does not invent a fixed “city bonus” or “city penalty” without block-level radiation, surface and wind data.

Why not call it skin temperature?

This is an environmental equivalent temperature for an exposed person, not a measurement of your body. Clothing, activity, sweating, health, acclimatization and time outside can all change what an individual actually experiences.

Primary method reference: the attached Real-Feel Skin Temperature Research framework (Tier 3 fallback). Benchmarks: UTCI background ↗ · Stull wet-bulb approximation ↗.

Models, timing & forecast policy

Models actually used

HRRR, ECMWF IFS and NOAA NBM are requested explicitly for the selected coordinates through Open-Meteo—not its automatic best-match model. Published initialization age, grid location, units and each hourly value are checked. These continuous point series may combine successive runs of the same named model; they are not a single pinned initialization. Verified native GRIB2 extracts remain a fallback for the two saved locations. Temperature, dew point, wind, gust and cloud cover use the requested lead-day weights wherever each field is available; humidity and feels-like are derived from those matching inputs. Pressure and visibility use only models publishing those fields. Current station readings remain observations. Official alerts, NWS rain probabilities and UV retain their own sources, because they are not interchangeable deterministic model quantities.

AI explains the evidence

The AI receives the blended numbers, individual model estimates, HRRR simulated reflectivity and latest local NWS discussion. It cannot change numeric cards or official alerts. Starting blend weights are not a statistically calibrated skill ranking.

Forecast confidence

Each daily Forecast confidence label is a relative index, not a probability. It starts with lead time and then uses the spread among NWS/model high temperatures, the spread in available rainfall guidance, the number of usable guidance sources, and whether official NWS day/night periods are present. Larger disagreement or missing coverage lowers confidence; a later day can rate better than an earlier day when the scientific guidance agrees better.

Precipitation windows

The main metric shows the next 24 hours. Daily cards show the remaining period through 7 AM, or complete 7 AM–7 AM future days. Totals are liquid equivalent, not measured rainfall. Interpolation within coarse model intervals does not establish storm arrival times.

Google WeatherNext is not currently included: approved forecast dataset access is required. A text-generation model is not a substitute for numerical weather data.

HRRR reflectivity is a forecast, not live radar. Its map uses a separately verified, timestamp-pinned NOAA HRRR tile source from Iowa State that covers the contiguous United States. ECMWF rain, NBM rain, temperature, wind and cloud raster maps are decoded from native contiguous-U.S. model data. Point-model availability is listed separately above.
